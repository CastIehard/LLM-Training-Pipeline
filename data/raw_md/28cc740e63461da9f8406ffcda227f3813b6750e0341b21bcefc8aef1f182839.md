        3. Major cities like [Berlin](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/berlin/), [Munich](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/munich/), [Frankfurt](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/frankfurt/), and [Hamburg](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/hamburg/) offer dynamic, international environments.

## Where to live: choosing your German city

Germany offers diverse living environments, from cosmopolitan cities to university towns and peaceful countryside. Your choice depends on priorities such as employment, budget, family needs, cultural scene, or environment.

**[Berlin](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/berlin/)** is a creative, international capital popular with startups, students, and freelancers.

**[Munich](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/munich/)** is a prosperous southern city hosting major corporate headquarters. Cost of living is high, but the city offers excellent security.

**[Frankfurt](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/frankfurt/)** is known for its financial center and major airport hub, with strong potential for international professionals.

**[Hamburg](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/hamburg/)** is a major port city that's dynamic and pleasant to live in.

**[Cologne](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/cologne/)** , **[Düsseldorf](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/dusseldorf/)** , and **[Stuttgart](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/stuttgart/)** are major economic regions with numerous opportunities in industry and services.

**[Dresden](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/dresden/)** is rapidly developing and more affordable.

## Visas and residency requirements

Moving to Germany requires completing specific administrative procedures. While the process is structured and transparent, understanding the requirements before arrival saves time and prevents complications.

### 🔑 Key requirements

**EU citizens don't need a visa** but must complete mandatory registration called **Anmeldung** at the local city hall within 14 days of arrival.

For **non-EU nationals, a[visa](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/912-work-visas-in-germany.html) or residence permit is required** before entering the country. Applications must be submitted at German embassies or consulates abroad.

The **Anmeldung is essential** for obtaining your tax number, opening a bank account, or signing a lease.

A **tax identification number** called **Steuer-ID** is automatically assigned after registration and is required for employment and tax filing.

**[Health insurance](https://www.expat.com/en/insurance/europe/germany/) is mandatory** for all residents and often required to obtain or extend residence permits.

## Working and doing business in Germany

Germany offers a stable, attractive job market open to international talent, particularly in technical and industrial sectors. The country also supports entrepreneurship, including for foreign nationals who meet specific administrative requirements.

### 🔑 What you need to know

**High-demand sectors** include engineering, healthcare, information technology, education, logistics, and construction.

German language skills are often required, except in large international companies or tech startups where English is the working language.

**Diploma recognition** is mandatory for regulated professions, particularly in healthcare and education.

[Starting a business in Germany](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/923-setting-a-business-in-germany.html) is possible as an expat but requires proof of business viability and registration with local authorities.

**Employment contracts are well-regulated** and provide strong employee protections.

## Higher education opportunities

**Germany is renowned for its high-quality higher education** , which is accessible, structured, and internationally oriented. The country attracts hundreds of thousands of international students annually, particularly due to low or no tuition fees and European degree recognition.

Institution types include universities (research-focused), **Hochschulen** , and **Fachhochschulen** (more professionally oriented).

**Instruction is primarily in German** , though increasing numbers of programs are offered in English, especially at master's level.

**Tuition fees are very low or non-existent** at public universities (except in Baden-Württemberg for non-EU nationals).

**Academic standards are internationally recognized** , particularly in engineering, sciences, medicine, business, and environmental studies.

**Admission requirements** include a recognized diploma such as **Abitur** or equivalent, sufficient language proficiency, and application through the uni-assist platform or directly to institutions.

## Banking and taxes in Germany

[Choose a bank](https://www.expat.com/en/bank/europe/germany/) that understands your expat needs.

[Learn more](https://www.expat.com/en/bank/europe/germany/)

Germany operates a reliable banking system and maintains clear tax regulations. Key procedures such as obtaining your **tax identification number** or **Steuer-ID** are essential from the start for employment, tax payment, and financial management.

### 🔑 Essential information

Germany has a **diverse banking network** including traditional banks such as Deutsche Bank, Commerzbank, and Sparkasse, plus online banks and digital banking options.

You can **open a bank account online or in branch**. Required documents typically include passport or ID card, proof of address through your **Anmeldung** , and sometimes proof of income.

**Credit and loans are accessible to foreigners** with good banking history and regular income. Interest rates are generally competitive.

A **tax number is mandatory** for working and filing tax returns. It's automatically assigned after you complete your registration at city hall.

**Income tax is withheld at source** from salaries. An annual tax return may be required depending on your situation.

**Tax classes** called **Steuerklassen** are determined by marital and family status and directly affect the amount of tax withheld.

## Healthcare and social protection

[Protect your health](https://www.expat.com/en/insurance/europe/germany/) during your expat journey.

[Get a quote](https://www.expat.com/en/insurance/europe/germany/)

Germany operates one of the world's most reputed healthcare systems, funded through mandatory insurance. Social protection is comprehensive, but accessing it requires completing specific procedures upon arrival.

### 🔑 What you need to know

**Health insurance is mandatory**. All residents must enroll in either public health insurance or private insurance.

Contribution amounts are calculated as a **percentage of your income** and shared between employer and employee.

Germany has an **extensive network of doctors** , clinics, and public or private hospitals, with generally short waiting times.

Social protection also includes unemployment insurance, pension, and workplace accident coverage, funded through mandatory contributions.

A **health insurance card** will be issued by your insurance provider, allowing direct access to healthcare services.

## Education and schools for expat families

Germany's education system is recognized for its quality and diversity but differs from systems in many other countries. Understanding how it works is essential for smoothly integrating children into school.

### 🔑 Key points

**School attendance is mandatory from ages 6 to 18** , with pathways adapted to students' abilities and plans.

**Public schools are free and high-quality** , with instruction primarily in German.

Many **international schools operate in major cities** , offering curricula in English or other languages, often with International Baccalaureate (IB) programs.

After primary school, students are directed to different types of institutions including **Gymnasium** , **Realschule** , or **Hauptschule**.

Language support is often provided for non-German-speaking children.

## Finding accommodation in Germany

Germany's rental market is highly regulated but competitive, especially in major cities. Thorough preparation and complete documentation are essential for securing accommodation quickly.

### 🔑 What you need to know

The **housing market is tight** in [Berlin](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/berlin/11565-accommodation-in-berlin.html), [Munich](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/munich/11561-accommodation-in-munich.html), [Hamburg](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/hamburg/11559-accommodation-in-hamburg.html), and [Frankfurt](https://www.expat.com/en/guide/europe/germany/frankfurt/11564-accommodation-in-frankfurt.html), where demand far exceeds supply.

To access rental properties, you'll need ID documents, proof of income, a credit certificate called **Schufa** , and sometimes a reference from your previous landlord. Leases are typically open-ended with a three-month notice period for tenants. Rent is quoted as either **Kaltmiete** (cold rent, excluding utilities) or **Warmmiete** (warm rent, including utilities but excluding electricity and internet).

**Property purchase is accessible to foreigners** but requires solid financing and often a substantial down payment.

Outdoor enthusiasts can enjoy hiking in the **Black Forest** , water sports on the lakes, and cycling on thousands of kilometers of dedicated paths.

Don't miss signature events like **Oktoberfest in Munich** , Christmas markets, and music and film festivals.

## Daily life in Germany

Living in Germany means experiencing an organized lifestyle where punctuality, efficiency, and respect for rules are cultural norms. Expats generally adapt quickly, especially in major cities with strong international communities.

### 🔑 Essential tips

Speaking German remains essential for full integration, though English is common in major cities and international companies.

The transportation network is reliable and punctual, with well-developed train, bus, tram, and cycling infrastructure.

Shops typically open from 9 AM to 8 PM and close on Sundays, except at train stations and airports.

Joining clubs and associations is highly recommended for integration.

## In brief

Germany's administrative system is structured with clear procedures to follow from arrival: the **Anmeldung** registration, choosing [health insurance](https://www.expat.com/en/insurance/europe/germany/), and obtaining your tax number.

The German economy is robust with strong demand in engineering, healthcare, IT, and education.

Germany offers professional opportunities for all profiles, from recent graduates to families and active retirees.

Quality of life is high, supported by excellent infrastructure, overall security, and a rich cultural environment.

Cost of living varies, higher in major southern and western cities, more affordable in the east.

German language skills are a major asset for integration, though English is present in certain professional environments.

**_Choosing Germany for relocation means opting for economic strength, excellent organization, and high quality of life. Whether you're coming to work, study, start a business, or join family, the country offers modern infrastructure, excellent healthcare, and an attractive job market.
